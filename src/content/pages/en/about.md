## In short

- Full-stack web developer with a year and a half of experience, moving into network engineering/security
- CCNA certified, currently building hands-on experience through containerlab labs (Nokia SR Linux, VyOS, OPNsense...) and studying for the FortiOS Administrator NSE4 exam
- Every lab is documented chronologically, with all the mistakes and debugging
- All configurations are publicly available on [GitHub](https://github.com/MilosGlamocak)

## Context

I'm creating this series to document my learning in the networking field. I'd describe my prior networking knowledge as technically thin but theoretically solid: I can find my way around various texts and understand the thought process behind a real-world solution. My technical understanding of IT comes primarily from my development background (a year and a half of full-stack web development). A good part of my current understanding of networks also comes from studying for the CCNA exam, which I recently passed.

Driven by the desire for a more stable and promising career, mainly because of the growing trend of replacing junior and mid-level programmers with AI models in a utopian pursuit of a cheaper yet more productive business model, I decided to steer my career toward the part of IT that I believe can't be largely replaced by artificial intelligence. After that statement I may look like a hypocrite, because I use AI every day myself, even as one of my main tools for learning and for getting my full-time web development job done. There's no escaping it, and it's true that it makes us more productive, but I'm afraid of the long-term price of that productivity. It's a deep topic that no single person can give a definitive answer to, so I won't spend much time on it.

## Preparation

Since I had no luck looking for a junior network engineer or junior network security job with my current CV, I realized that HR teams don't care much about my programming background or the fact that I formally have some networking knowledge. It makes sense that hands-on networking experience comes first, but that's the classic catch-22 of needing experience to get a job while needing a job to get experience.

That's why I came up with the idea of creating a collection of containerlab projects in which I show my thought process when configuring real network topologies to a potential employer. Everything I'll use here is new to me: the only network operating system I've worked with is Cisco IOS and a little FortiOS, and I've never used Nokia SR Linux, VyOS, OPNsense and similar ones. I chose them because they're free to use and I hear they're used in real environments too.

I'll use draw.io to create illustrations of the network topologies and for planning, and I'll publish all my configurations to a public GitHub repository so the labs can be recreated on other machines.

Since I have no experience with containerlab or Nokia SR Linux, the first few labs will be a simple introduction to their features and how they work.

I picked SR Linux as the primary L3 device for my labs because it's open (not "open-source open"), free, and I've heard it's used in production environments around the world. The alternative I found is VyOS, but I would have to run it as a full virtual machine, so for now Nokia SRL is my favorite.
